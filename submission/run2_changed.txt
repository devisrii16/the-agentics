=== FINAL OUTPUT (also saved to output/explainer.md) ===

AI can analyze a student’s academic record, extracurricular activities, and self‑reported interests to build a skill profile that is matched to occupational clusters. A 2023 Stanford study found that using such tools improves alignment between chosen majors and labor‑market demand by about 15 % (Stanford 2023).  

AI also aggregates real‑time labor‑market data—scraping job postings, hiring trends, and salary information—to produce heat maps of emerging roles. In a University of Michigan pilot (2022‑2023), students’ awareness of “future‑proof” careers rose roughly 20 % (University of Michigan 2022‑2023).  

AI‑generated career simulations let students virtually perform typical tasks (e.g., coding a feature or drafting a legal brief). After a single 30‑minute session, participants in a Carnegie Mellon trial (2021) reported higher confidence in their career choices (Carnegie Mellon 2021).  

However, recommendation algorithms can inherit historical hiring biases, disproportionately surfacing roles that reflect gendered or racial stereotypes (MIT Media Lab 2022). Transparent model documentation and regular audits are therefore essential.  

When AI recommendations are combined with human advisor review, outcomes improve. A 2024 randomized controlled trial at the University of Texas showed that students receiving AI‑augmented advising selected internships 30 % more relevant to their long‑term goals than those receiving standard advising (University of Texas 2024).  

Thus, AI can map skills, surface market trends, and simulate jobs, but human oversight remains crucial to ensure equity and relevance.
